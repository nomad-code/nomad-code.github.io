reading the surface

  • Approach is the deepest valley — ~40% of the gap to scratch lives here, and it gets worse the longer the shot. That's the lane the flick fix pays off in.
  • Off the tee pokes above the plane on the two longest bins — face control, not distance, is holding up.
  • Around the green sits just under the plane on the short bins — quiet hands turn these red cells green fast.
  • Putting hovers at the plane — the smallest lever. Don't let a flat lane steal time from the approach valley.

Click a bar to load its stratum here. Step the sessions to watch the valley fill in as you drill one fault at a time.

◆ sample sim rounds

This is illustrative data shaped like a real Spica log — six weekly sim sessions on one GSPro course, strokes gained bucketed by carry distance and by stratum. Replace the SESSIONS array with your own per-shot SG (start-to-hole baseline minus finish-to-hole minus one, bucketed) and the surface becomes yours. The story it's built to tell is the population prior from Broadie's Every Shot Counts: about two-thirds of the gap to scratch is the long game, and approach is the single deepest lane.
