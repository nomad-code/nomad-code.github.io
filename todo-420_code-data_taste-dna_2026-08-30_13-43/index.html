What the full set actually says

The middle still agrees. The ends do not: he reserves 2.6% of films for the top band against 6.4% of albums, and puts 4.4% of films on the floor against 0.4% of albums. He is far stingier with films and far more willing to condemn one.

The likeliest reason is not taste but selection. Albums are sought out — 2 of 456 sit at or below 4.9, because a record he disliked mostly never got rated. Films arrive: you get taken to them. 31 of 699 sit at or below 1.5★, and one is Birdemic: Shock and Terror.

That has a consequence the earlier pass missed. Photographs are captured the way films are watched — in bulk, often without deliberation — so the film curve may be the better model for them than the album curve currently in use. The scores in the other artifact are built on the album curve. Changing it would move every photo, so it is flagged here, not silently swapped.

Three witnesses, one timeline

Each column is an independent record of the same years, kept by a different app for a different reason. Where they disagree is where the interesting questions are — 2024 has the most workouts ever recorded and almost no photographs.

Read the zeros as gaps, not as behaviour. Songs stop after 2023 because the Spotify export was taken on 2024-04-29 and nothing has replaced it; he did not stop listening. Workouts start in 2018 because that is when the Strava account does. The photo dip in 2023–25 is the seam between the Google archive (ends 2023-07) and the live library. Three incomplete records that fail in different places is exactly why more than one witness is worth keeping.

The DNA — what fits on what

This is the payoff of putting everything on one axis. A 9.3 photo and a 9.3 song are equally rare by construction, so they can compete for the same megabyte and packing a device becomes sort-and-fill — no per-medium fudge factor.

Sizes are measured, not assumed: 1.11 MB mean per photo over 1,963 real archive files, 0.06 MB for a watch-sized render, 4 MB per song at 256k AAC, 0.05 MB for a film's poster and identity. A film cannot live on a watch; its name can. The device budgets are the one genuinely speculative input, and are stated so they can be argued with.

The whole rated corpus is 70 GB

Which means the vault isn't the problem — M5 swallows everything with 3.3 TB spare. The squeeze is entirely at the small end. The watch keeps things rated 8.7 and above; the phone drops to 7.0. That cutoff is the answer to "what am I taking" at each size.

5,423 liked songs are unrated and are counted but never promoted. Inventing a score for them would put unearned material on the smallest device — the exact failure this project keeps catching.

What's still wrong

Open

The album curve may be the wrong ruler for photographs. See the correction at the top. Films and photos share a capture pattern that albums don't.

Songs are rated by album, not individually. 3,154 of 8,577 liked tracks inherit a score from a rated album; the rest have only the like. A per-track rating would need a model he hasn't built by hand, so it isn't faked here.

Device budgets are guesses. 2 GB on the watch and 32 GB on the phone are plausible, not measured. Every cutoff above moves if they change.

Nothing has been copied, moved or deleted. Tiers are computed. Enforcing them needs a verified second copy to exist first, and there is still no backup of any kind.
