What to think while riding

1

Set power + cadence

Choose the session’s watts or %FTP and hold 85–95 rpm seated. Avoid low-cadence torque, standing starts, and forceful toe-off while the fracture is healing.

2

Wait for the signal

HR commonly lags workload by roughly 30–90 seconds. Ignore it during a one-minute all-out; read the last half of longer reps.

3

Use it as a guardrail

Same watts + higher HR can mean heat, fatigue, dehydration, or lost efficiency. Same HR + higher watts is progress. Power remains the score.

Ice-bath HR: measure, do not chase

Is sub-40 plausible?

Yes. From a resting 47 bpm, cold facial immersion and breath-hold can trigger a dive-reflex drop below 40. But lower is not “fitter,” and cold also raises sympathetic load and can provoke rhythm irregularity. Never extend a breath-hold or stay in solely to win the number.

Capture a clean five-minute trace

  1. Start an Apple Watch Other / open-goal workout one minute before entry; snug the band.
  2. Enable Water Lock. Keep breathing normally; note water temperature and whether the face was immersed.
  3. Stay recording for three recovery minutes after exit. Apple Watch samples HR continuously during workouts and recovery.
  4. Allow Strava to read Heart Rate + Workouts from Apple Health; confirm the trace appears before treating it as data.
Safety: exit for chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, confusion, or uncontrolled breathing. Do not do breath-hold cold immersion alone. Optical-watch readings can contain isolated high/low anomalies.
