the loop · how you actually start00 / start here

Don't try to "get good at golf." That's a fog. The loop below is the whole method — it fits one sim session and it tells you, every time, the single thing worth working on next. Everything else on this page is just detail behind these five moves.

the weekly loop · one sim session · ~45 min

Measure → find the leak → fix one thing → refilm → log it.

measure — Play a full round on one GSPro course (Mammoth Dunes or DPC Pebble). Let the Spica score every shot. Don't judge it — just let the data land.
find the leak — Bucket the round into the four strata (tee / approach / around green / putting). Your worst stratum this round is the leak. It's usually approach — that's not a coincidence, it's the population prior below.
fix one thing — Pick one swing fault (grip, lead knee, or the flick) that feeds that leak. Film it down-the-line and face-on. One fault. Never a checklist mid-swing.
refilm — Drill it, then film the same shot again. Put the two clips side by side. The delta is the whole point — a beautiful swing is a diff you can see, like the surf clip.
log it — One line in the journal: date, course, worst stratum, the fault you drilled, the score delta. Over weeks this becomes your real strokes-gained profile — better than any generic tip.
Why it works: the sim removes the two things that stall most golfers — no guessing where you lost strokes (the score already knows), and no waiting for a range trip to test a change (next shot is one click away). You're running a tight measure-fix loop most players can't. Protect it: one variable at a time.
strokes-gained ontology · the biggest ask01 / the map

Strokes gained is one idea: every shot is worth a fraction of a stroke, and a good shot is one that leaves you better off than an average shot from the same spot. Your Spica already computes this per shot — that per-shot "score" is strokes gained. The power comes from bucketing thousands of those scores into four strata, so you can see where your strokes leak, not just that they do.

strokes_gained = (avg strokes to hole out from where you started) − (avg strokes from where the shot finished) − 1   ·   + = beat the field   − = lost ground
where your strokes actually leakbogey golfer → scratch

This is the population prior (Broadie, Every Shot Counts): for a ~90s shooter closing the gap to scratch, here's roughly how the lost strokes split by stratum. Your own sim data overwrites this — but until it does, this is where to point practice. Note how small putting is versus the myth.

The headline: "drive for show, putt for dough" is mostly wrong for amateurs. Two-thirds of the gap to scratch is the long game — full swings, tee and approach. That's also exactly the part the Spica measures best, and the part your swing work (grip / knee / flick) directly improves. The leverage and the swing fix point at the same place.

the swing as positions · watch the hot framesP1 → P10

Every swing passes through the same checkpoints. You don't fix a "swing" — you fix a frame. The highlighted nodes are where your three faults live, so those are the frames to scrub to when you review a clip.

Filming protocol: two angles, phone at hip height. Down-the-line (behind you, on the target line) shows plane, the flick, and lead-knee movement. Face-on shows grip, weight shift, and shaft lean at impact. Same two angles every time so clips are comparable week to week — that's what makes the diff real.

Can I get the videos?swing capture
The Spica/GolfJoy setup captures shot data, not swing video by default — for that, add your own camera (phone on a small tripod, DTL + face-on). Apps like Onform or Hudl Technique auto-record, slow-mo, and let you draw lines and overlay two clips. That's your "get the videos" pipeline: camera → Onform → side-by-side diff. Check whether the GolfJoy app itself exposes a clip export before buying anything.
How well does it sync with Trackman?data trust
Trackman (dual radar) is the tour-lab gold standard, so treat it as the reference, not the Spica. The Spica is a strong consumer/camera-based monitor — excellent for relative tracking (is my club path improving?) and shot scoring, but expect some offset on absolute numbers (spin especially) vs Trackman. For the loop that's fine — you care about your own deltas over time, and the Spica is consistent with itself. Only chase Trackman-level precision if you're fitting clubs.

north star · shot traces on real geography04 / the dream

GSPro courses × Esri 3D × strokes-gained shot traces.

You floated tying the courses to Esri data like TECH-481 · esri-3d — but with shot traces and strokes gained. That's the real vision: not a spreadsheet of numbers, but your rounds drawn onto the actual terrain. Here's the stack.

1Terrain — Esri 3D world elevation + imagery for the real course (Pebble, Mammoth Dunes). The ground truth GSPro already laser-scanned.
2Shot traces — each shot from the Spica (start x,y → carry → finish) drawn as an arc on the terrain. Every round you've played, layered.
3Strokes-gained heat — color each trace by its SG score. Red arcs = where you bled strokes on that hole, green = where you gained. The leak becomes a place you can see.
4Stratum overlay — filter to tee / approach / around-green / putting. Watch your approach misses cluster short-right on the terrain, not just in a table.
Feasibility: the data exists (Spica shot logs + Esri ArcGIS 3D APIs + GSPro's course geometry). This is a real project when you want it — it belongs in bpo, personal. The loop above works today with zero of this built; the Esri map is the beautiful surface you earn once the log has rounds in it.

The whole thing rests on one habit: one measured round, one filmed fault, one logged line — every week. Do that and in three months you'll have a personal strokes-gained profile, a folder of side-by-side swing diffs, and a swing that looks the way you want. Not from a tip. From the loop.
