01 · hardware paradigmHID vs MIDI CC

Basic USB-HID pedals emulate keystrokes — dead simple, but they scale like keybindings: every action needs a unique chord, and the OS can't tell two pedals apart. MIDI Continuous Controller signals carry a channel + CC number + 0–127 value, so banks re-map the same physical switch to a fresh logical address on demand. That's the difference between 10 actions and 300.

02 · OS routing & emulation layersignal → action

A pedal press is a class-compliant MIDI event. A small userspace daemon — pedald — subscribes to the MIDI sequencer, resolves (channel, CC, bank) against a profile, and fans out to two routes: A the audio graph, B the model / context layer. Nothing touches the keyboard event stream, so it never collides with typing.

03 · model-per-pedal architecturelanes & state

Assign independent pedals to independent models or tasks by making each pedal a named tmux session — a "lane" — with its own state machine. The pedal press arms the lane; the lane's LED, driven back over MIDI OUT, is the state contract. Expression CC then acts on whichever lane is armed (gain on an audio lane, top-k on a vector lane).

Lane state machine

Each lane is one of five states; the LED colour is the contract, so status is glanceable without looking at a screen.

idle→ armed→ busy→ ok /err

Lanes 1–3 → local vector-DB searches · Lane 4 → audio-to-text (PTT whisper, CC 23) · Lane 5 → execution routing. LEDs unlit = idle; lime = armed; amber pulse = busy; green = ok; red = err.

Expression axis

The expression pedal emits CC 11 continuously, mapped onto the armed lane:

armed laneCC 11 controls
audiogain stage, −∞…+6 dB
vector searchtop-k (1…40 results)
model gatewaytemperature / thinking budget

One physical sweep means three different things depending on which lane holds the arm — that's the whole economy of the design.
