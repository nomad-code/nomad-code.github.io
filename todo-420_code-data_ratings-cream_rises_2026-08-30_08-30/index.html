Your curve, transplanted

One second channel per tool

Letterboxd offers both a rating and a heart. Across every entry I can read you used the heart 0 times — never. But 10 are marked rewatch.

In Apple Photos it's the exact inverse: the heart is the only tool you touch. Same person, two apps, and in each you adopt precisely one channel beyond the score and ignore the other. So the channel is real and the form is incidental — which means reading the Photos heart as your second channel isn't an assumption, it's the pattern.

Rewatched films do rate above once-watched ones, but on ten films that's a direction, not a result. Flagged, not claimed.

A rating is a coordinate

Not a number — a datapoint with a cascade of specificity, like 2026 → 2026-08 → 2026-08-30T12:16:03.412Z, or a lat/long at 1 km versus 1 m. Every rung is a legitimate value. A date with only a year still sorts, still filters, still joins; it joins coarsely, and it says so.

That forced a correction. The previous pass handed every frame a crisp integer whether six signals backed it or none — writing 6.2 for a frame we know nothing about claims a discrimination we can't make. So precision is now part of the value and has to be earned, and like a truncated timestamp each rating denotes an interval: 7.4 means [7.35, 7.45), 7 means [6.5, 7.5).

Two limits, smaller wins

The curve. Your 456 albums don't discriminate evenly. Through the crowded 60–79 stretch one album moves you 0.006 on the 0–10 axis, so tenths there are real. At the floor it's 0.056. The scale does not carry uniform resolution, and smoothing over that is the same error as the recency proxy: a real gradient in the evidence, laundered into a clean number.

The frame. How many independent signals actually fired on it. A frame carried only by a tie-break gets a whole number; decimals on a tie-break are noise wearing a lab coat.

The ceiling is a band, not a point

293 frames tie at exactly 10, because your curve holds only 29 albums across 90–100. That tie is the scale being honest about its own top end.

This rests on 50 films, not 699

Letterboxd's HTML is behind Cloudflare's JS challenge — the ratings page, the profile, every paginated view returns 403. The public RSS feed is open but hard-capped at 50 entries, so that's the sample: the 50 most recently logged films, which at least spans 2014–2026 rather than being purely recent. The agreement above is strong for n=50 and it is still n=50.

One concrete consequence: your histogram has real mass at 0.5★ and 1★, but the sample never drew one, so those bands got zero frames and T0 is smaller than it should be. Settings → Data → Export drops a ratings.csv with all 699 — put it at projects/ratings/export/ and the whole thing recomputes against the real distribution.

Two traps, both caught

Google's imageViews is not a play count

It looked like the perfect un-fakeable signal — engagement, produced by looking rather than tagging. But it decays almost monotonically with recency: mean 55.8 for 2001 against 15.6 for 2022. It accumulates. Used raw it would have ranked your archive by age and called it taste. It's now z-scored within its own year, so it can only ever say "looked at more than its contemporaries".

My own "curated" signal was recency in disguise

I gave a bonus to anything in the live Photos library, reasoning it had survived forward. It hadn't — the live library is simply recent. It pushed 57% of 2025 above 90 while 2022 sat at 3.6%. The term is gone, and every frame is now ranked against its own year, so each era contributes its own cream at the same rate. That is what "cream rises" has to mean across a whole life rather than a recent one.

A day is an album

You rate albums, not tracks — and the photo corpus has the same shape, it was just never named. A burst is a take; a place-day is a session; a calendar event is a sitting. Those are the albums, frames are the tracks, and nobody rates tracks. An album scores at the 90th percentile of its members, not the mean, because a great record is carried by its peaks and averaging would punish a brilliant afternoon that also held nine boring frames.

This is your Dijkstra point in the form it actually applies: a frame adjacent to something valuable becomes cheaper to reach. A mediocre shot from an extraordinary day outranks an identical shot from an ordinary one — because what you're retrieving is the day, and the frame is the way in. 33,898 frames were lifted this way.

Standing somewhere changes what you carry

The intrinsic score is fixed. Context is a second term, added at query time and never written back:

final = base_lifted + context(where you are, what day it is)

Nothing is re-rated when you travel; things are re-carried. A mediocre Heathrow photo doesn't become good when you land — it becomes temporarily worth having offline. Landing in London promotes 223 frames into phone tier; Tokyo promotes 724. Same formula, one term recomputed, no separate "geo mode".

The screenshot tell, and the Watch

277 annotated screenshots

The pattern you described — screenshot something, then mark it to show where your eye went — is recorded by Photos as an adjustment on a detected screenshot. It's the single highest-intent act in the corpus: you went back to something and pointed at it. Weighted second only to a favourite.

Against that, 14,814 frames are burst also-rans and 4,876 sit inside screenshot documentation runs. Both are pushed down, neither is deleted.

Watch as a third witness

238 workout routes, 323,601 trackpoints at 1 Hz and roughly one-metre accuracy — better positioning than any other source you have, across 78 days.

It fires exactly where photos and calendar are both blind: the 6am run, the long layover, the first morning somewhere new. From this single 3.5-month export it upgrades 22 days of the country ontology, 7 of which had no country at all.

What this doesn't do yet

Timeline membership isn't wired in

Your strongest curation act — pulling something forward into the ~6 GB-per-year Timeline tier — is still a folder on disk, not a per-frame join. That's the single biggest available signal still unused, and it's the honest reason the curated term is absent rather than approximated.

The Google corpus is archive-only

All 41,036 sidecars read archived: true, so the flag carries no contrast here — this export is the archive tier. The album/timeline half lives in a different export and would give the curated/dumped split real teeth.

Nothing has been moved or deleted

Tiers are computed, not enforced. No file was copied to the M5, no byte removed from the M3. That step needs your word, and it needs a verified second copy to exist first.
